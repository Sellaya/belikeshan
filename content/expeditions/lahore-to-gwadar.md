## The first long ride

After years of northern Pakistan stretches — Skardu, the high passes and mountain roads that built the foundation — this was the **first longest solo trip** I undertook across the length of the country.

**5,000 kilometres. 10 days. February 1 to February 10, 2022.**

Lahore to Gwadar, and all the way down to **Jiwni** — the western edge of Pakistan's coast near the Iranian border.

## On the motorcycle

Solo on an adventure motorcycle, fully loaded for self-supported travel. Long days averaging **500 kilometres** — heat in the interior, wind on the coast, and road surfaces that changed every few hundred kilometres.

**Road conditions varied dramatically:**
- Smooth motorway sections through Punjab
- Heavy traffic approaching Karachi
- Open coastal highway with long empty stretches
- Rough patches, sand drift near the coast, and sharp turns cut into cliff faces along the Makran

Riding in February meant manageable temperatures in the south — but the Makran still demands respect. A loaded bike, full riding gear, and the discipline to read the road ahead.

## Karachi — before the coast

Before turning west onto the Makran Coastal Highway, Karachi offered a pause in the intensity of the ride:

- **Sea View (Clifton)** — the Arabian Sea at the city's edge
- **Mazar-e-Quaid** — paying respects at the mausoleum of Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah
- **Abdullah Shah Ghazi shrine** — a moment of reflection and connection to the city
- **Burns Road** — some of the best food in Karachi. Biryani, kebabs, and the kind of meal that fuels the next thousand kilometres

## The Makran Coastal Highway

One of the **best terrains in Pakistan** — and one of the most unforgiving.

The Makran Coastal Highway (N-10) runs along the Arabian Sea through Balochistan, linking Karachi to Gwadar. It is among the most scenic roads in the country: turquoise water on one side, golden cliffs and desert on the other.

### Landmarks along the route

- **Hingol National Park** — dramatic rock formations rising from the desert, including the mud volcanoes and the Sphinx-like formation
- **Princess of Hope** — the natural rock sculpture shaped by wind and time, standing alone against the horizon
- **Kund Malir Beach** — where the highway meets open sand and the sea
- **Ormara** — a coastal stop along the cliff road
- **Pasni** — fishing port and gateway further west
- **Gwadar** — the port city at the heart of the CPEC corridor, where the journey's name ends but the road continues
- **Jiwni** — the farthest point, near the border, where the land runs out and the sea takes over

## The crash — diesel on the road

On the Makran Coastal Highway, the road surface turned treacherous when **diesel spilled from plastic gallons** carried by passing traffic. The surface became slick without warning.

The bike went down. Gear took the impact. No serious injury — but a clear reminder that on the Makran, conditions change in an instant. Picked up, checked the bike, and kept riding.

## Why this journey mattered

This was not the USA Loop. It was earlier, rawer, and entirely within Pakistan — the ride that proved long-distance solo travel was possible after years of northern adventures.

It built the confidence, the road reading, and the endurance that every later expedition — including the 16,000-kilometre USA Loop — would depend on.

**Lahore to Gwadar. 5,000 km. 10 days. The Makran, Karachi, Jiwni, and everything in between.**
